A desk that picks up when you cannot.

Rivetory is built in Meridian, Idaho, by Calvin Thurman. One person, one promise, one product.

Why a desk, not an app.

Small companies lose jobs to missed calls. Not because they are bad at the work, but because the phone rings at 2am and nobody is there to answer it. Hiring a night dispatcher is out of reach for a company with a handful of techs. An answering service takes a message and hopes someone calls back.

Rivetory is the middle that did not exist: a desk that answers, takes the job, books the visit and tells the owner, every time, at a price a small company can carry.

Why restoration first.

Water and fire emergencies happen at night, the jobs are large, and the intake questions are specific. If the desk can handle a basement filling with water at 1am, it can handle anything. Restoration is where a missed call costs the most, so it is where the desk proves itself first.

On AI, plainly.

AI is a touchy subject, and plenty of people are wary of it. Fair enough. The desk uses it the way a good dispatcher uses a phone: to answer, to listen, to write it down and to pass it on. It says what it is at the start of every call. It does not pretend to be a person. The point is not the technology. The point is the job you would have lost.

The standard.

Every call gets answered. Every emergency gets escalated now. Every owner hears about it within a minute. If the desk misses, the customer hears about it from us first, and the next month is on us when the guarantee applies.
