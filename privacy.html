●  Privacy

Privacy, in plain words.

Last updated November 21, 2026. Rivetory stores only what the job needs, and nothing else is the point.

Calls are recorded.

The desk announces at the start of every call that it is an automated assistant answering for the company, and that the call is recorded. Recording exists so the owner can check what was said, so a mishandled call can be reviewed, and so the booking is accurate.

Recordings are kept only while the job record needs them, then deleted. They are not sold, not shared with advertisers, and not used to train models for other companies.

What the desk collects on a call.

Name, callback number, address of the damage, what happened, and insurance carrier if the caller knows it. That is the intake, and it is sent to the company that hired the desk. Nothing else is collected from a caller.

What the owner receives.

A text and an email summary of every call, plus a weekly recap. Summaries go only to the contact details the owner gave at setup.

Texts.

The desk texts a caller only about their own request, for example a booking confirmation or a change to the visit time. A caller who replies STOP gets no further texts. No marketing texts are sent to callers.

This website.

Visiting this site does not create an account and does not send us your name or number. If you book a setup call through the booking link, the booking provider receives the details you enter there. If you email us, we keep the email only to answer it.

Who else sees the data.

The companies that run the phone lines, the booking tool and the email delivery see the data they need to do their part, and no more. Rivetory does not sell caller or customer data to anyone.

Your choices.

An owner can ask for their company's data, ask for a correction, or ask for deletion by emailing services@rivetory.com. A caller can ask what was kept from their call the same way.

Changes.

If this policy changes, the date at the top changes with it, and customers are told before a change that affects their data takes effect.
