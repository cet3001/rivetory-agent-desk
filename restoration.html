●  For restoration companies

Water and fire do not wait for business hours.

A supply line breaks at 1am. A kitchen fire fills the house with smoke at 11pm. The first company that picks up gets the job. The rest get a voicemail.

Built for the call you cannot miss.

Restoration is the first vertical because the stakes are the clearest. An after-hours call is usually an emergency, the job is usually large, and the caller is usually deciding between you and whoever answers next.

The desk is built for independent companies with 2 to 50 people. No franchise phone rules, no call center script. Your fact sheet, your service area, your on-call tech, your rules.

The intake questions the desk asks.

  • Name and callback number
  • Address of the damage
  • What happened, water or fire
  • How long ago it started
  • Is water still running, is anyone unsafe
  • Insurance carrier, if they know it

Short questions, in that order. Enough to send a tech. Nothing the caller has to repeat in the morning.

●  Emergencies

When it is active water or fire, nobody waits.

  1. 1

    Confirmed.

    The desk confirms active water or fire in the first minute of the call.

  2. 2

    Tech alerted.

    Your on-call tech is called and texted immediately, with the address and the details.

  3. 3

    Caller guided.

    The caller is told what to do until the tech arrives, and when to expect them.

Non-emergencies get a booked visit or a morning callback, not a 2am wake-up. The desk knows the difference.

●  Who it fits

Independent companies, 2 to 50 people.

  • Water damage, fire damage, or both. The desk knows the intake for each.
  • An owner who answers the phone during the day. The desk covers the hours you cannot.
  • An on-call tech or a rotation. Emergencies need a name and a number, not a queue.
  • Insurance work. The carrier is captured on the first call, so the file starts clean.
