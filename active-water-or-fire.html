What happens when a caller reports active water or fire?

Nobody waits. The desk confirms active water or fire in the first minute, calls and texts your on-call tech immediately with the address and the details, and tells the caller what to do until the tech arrives.

●  Questions

Short answers.

What counts as an emergency?

Active water or fire. The desk confirms it in the first minute of the call.

Who gets called first?

Your on-call tech, by call and text, with the address and the details. Emergencies need a name and a number, not a queue.

What is the caller told while they wait?

What to do until the tech arrives, and when to expect them.

What happens to a call that is not an emergency?

Routine jobs get a visit booked. Non-emergencies get a booked visit or a morning callback, not a 2am wake-up. The desk knows the difference.

Do I get woken up for every call?

No. Non-emergencies get a booked visit or a morning callback. Emergencies go to your on-call tech first, and you get the summary within a minute.

What if I have no on-call tech?

Emergencies need a name and a number. The desk is built for companies with an on-call tech or a rotation.

What happens when a customer calls my restoration company after hours?

Your line forwards to the desk when you do not pick up. The desk answers, takes the job details, and sends you a summary within a minute.

What questions does the desk ask a caller?

Six short questions, in the same order every time: name, address, what happened, how long ago, safety, and insurance carrier.

How fast do I hear about a call?

Within a minute, by text and email, with the caller's number, what happened, and the booking.
